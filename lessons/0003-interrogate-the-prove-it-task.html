Lesson 3 · Choose the oracle

Interrogate the Prove It task

A valuable product question is not automatically a good verification task. The task needs an oracle we can defend.

Candidate A

Judge reasoning provenance

Catch when GAiDFLY introduces an idea that the debrief later attributes to the student.

PRODUCT RELEVANCE: HIGH

ORACLE STRENGTH: LOW TODAY

The checker must decide whether two differently worded ideas are materially the same. A deterministic matcher misses paraphrases; another model judge reintroduces drift.

Candidate B

Bind the evidence surface

Require the exact paper and brief, bind the debrief to the exact transcript, and refuse missing or changed inputs.

PRODUCT RELEVANCE: MEDIUM-HIGH

ORACLE STRENGTH: HIGH

Hashes, required files, speaker turns, and refusal states are independently checkable. This creates trustworthy evidence for later human provenance review.

Attack Candidate A

AttackCheapest false pass
Already greenAssert that the prompt contains “never attribute GAiDFLY contributions” without testing a debrief result.
Fixture overfitRecognize the visible examples or exact repeated phrases while missing paraphrased adoption.
Case omissionTest obvious copying but omit embedded-answer questions, hints, student extensions, and paper-derived ideas.
Checker weaknessVerify that turn references exist without checking whether they support the provenance claim.
NondeterminismPass once under a model judge even though repeated runs disagree.

Candidate A becomes strong only after we have a credible oracle. Building that oracle may be the research project, not the first harness exercise.
